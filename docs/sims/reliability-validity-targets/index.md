## Description

A rubric can be steady without being right. **Reliable** means scores land close together, so reviewers agree. **Valid** means scores land on the bullseye, so they measure what matters. This poster shows four archery targets, each with a bullseye and six shots, one for each combination.

In **Explore** mode you hover or click a target to read what it means for a rubric. In **Quiz me** mode you read six scenarios and click the target that matches. The poster is a drawing made for this book (SVG). Its shot positions are fixed and it prints no numbers.

| Target | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reliable and valid | Scores are consistent and measure the right thing. |
| Reliable but not valid | Scores are consistent but miss what matters. |
| Valid on average but not reliable | Scores scatter, though their average points the right way. |
| Neither reliable nor valid | Scores scatter and miss what matters. |

### Controls

- **Explore and Quiz me buttons:** switch modes. Quiz me starts a new run.
- **The four targets:** hover, focus with the Tab key, or click. In quiz mode a click is your answer.
- **Next:** moves to the next scenario.
- **Play again:** repeats the quiz as practice.

### Good to Know

Only your first committed answer on the first completed run counts as evidence of mastery. Practice runs give the same feedback but do not change that first-run score. Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

- Each scenario has one attempt. Only the first completed run counts as evidence.
- The poster prints its own target titles, so the sim adds no labels over it.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner can **classify** each of six rubric scenarios as reliable and valid, reliable but not valid, valid on average but not reliable, or neither, with at least 5 of 6 correct on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Understand.)

### Grade Level

Adult learners: teachers (K-12 and college), instructional designers, librarians, and OER coordinators.

### Duration

5-10 minutes.

### Prerequisites

- The terms *rubric reliability* and *rubric validity* from [Chapter 3](../../chapters/03-ten-metric-rubric-scoring/index.md).

### Activities

1. **Explore (3 min).** Hover over each target. Which two have shots close together?
2. **Classify (4 min).** Press **Quiz me** and classify all six scenarios.
3. **Invent (3 min).** Write a rubric scenario of your own for the "reliable but not valid" target.

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer by using it.

1. Two reviewers always agree, but the scores do not predict learning. Which target? *(Reliable but not valid.)*
2. Scores vary widely, but their average tracks student performance. Which target? *(Valid on average but not reliable.)*
3. Is a rubric with scattered scores useless? *(No. If the average is on target, more reviewers or clearer descriptors can help.)*
4. Can agreement alone prove a rubric is right? *(No. Reviewers can agree and still miss what matters.)*

### Connection to the OER Evaluator rubric

A trustworthy rubric needs both properties. [Chapter 14](../../chapters/14-checking-agent-output/index.md) measures agreement, and [Chapter 17](../../chapters/17-ab-testing-analysis/index.md) compares scores with student results.

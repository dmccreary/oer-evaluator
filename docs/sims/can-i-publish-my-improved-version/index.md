## Description

An agent's edited copy is a derivative work, so the license must be read before the first edit. This poster answers one practical question for each license in the Chapter 15 table: may you publish an improved version, and what must you do? The cards run from most to least permissive. Each has the same rows: the license name, a verdict, and the duties. The verdict has three states, shown with a symbol and a word as well as color: yes, yes with conditions, and no. A footer band covers the last row of the table: with no license, all rights are reserved, so ask the owner first. The poster prints the license names as text and does not redraw the official Creative Commons icons.

This MicroSim is a **grid overlay**: the poster is a drawing made for this book (SVG), and each zone is a clickable rectangle over it.

| Zone | What it shows |
|---|---|
| CC0 | Yes: Nothing is required, and giving credit is good practice. |
| CC BY | Yes, with conditions: Credit the author, link to the license, and say that you changed the work. |
| CC BY-SA | Yes, with conditions: The same as CC BY, and release your version under CC BY-SA. |
| CC BY-NC | Yes, without commercial use: The same as CC BY, and do not sell or use it for commercial gain. |
| CC BY-NC-SA | Yes, without commercial use: The same as CC BY, release under CC BY-NC-SA, and do not use it commercially. |
| CC BY-ND | No: You may not share a changed version. You may make changes for your own private use only. |
| No license, all rights reserved | No. Ask the owner for permission first. |

### Controls

- **Explore and Quiz Me buttons:** switch modes. Quiz Me starts a new run.
- **The zones:** hover over a zone or press it to read its summary and facts in the panel below the poster.
- **Quiz questions:** read the question, then press the zone that answers it. A wrong press lets you try again.
- **Try Again:** repeats the quiz after the last question.

### Good to Know

Quiz mode scores each question as you answer it. The MicroSim does not store your answers. The poster prints its own headings, so the sim adds no labels on top of it.

- The chapter's table has no row for CC BY-NC-ND, so the poster has none either. The author decides whether to add that row to the chapter and the poster together.
- Add the official Creative Commons icons only by following Creative Commons' guidance on its marks. This poster uses text.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner can **decide** whether an improved version of a resource may be published under its license, and **state** what you must do. (Bloom level: Apply.)

### Grade Level

Adult learners: teachers (K-12 and college), instructional designers, librarians, and OER coordinators.

### Duration

5-10 minutes.

### Prerequisites

- The six Creative Commons licenses from [Chapter 2](../../chapters/02-open-licenses-attribution/index.md) and derivative works from [Chapter 15](../../chapters/15-improving-resources-with-ai/index.md).

### Activities

1. **Explore (3 min).** Press each card from left to right. Where does the answer change from yes to no?
2. **Quiz (3 min).** Press **Quiz Me** and match four situations to the cards.
3. **Apply (4 min).** Find the license on a resource you want to improve. Read its card and write the notice you would add to your version.

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer against the poster.

1. A resource is CC BY-ND. May you publish your improved version? *(No. You may make changes for your own private use only.)*
2. A resource is CC BY-SA. What must you do? *(The same as CC BY, and release your version under CC BY-SA.)*
3. A resource has no license. May you publish an improved version? *(No. All rights are reserved, so ask the owner for permission first.)*

### Connection to the OER Evaluator rubric

This poster saves effort by stopping work on a resource that cannot be legally shared once improved. Read the license before the first edit.

## References

1. [Derivative work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_work) - Wikipedia - Explains how a work based on another is treated in copyright law. Background for the chapter's check of what a license allows before an agent changes a resource.
2. [Marking Your Work with a CC License](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Marking_your_work_with_a_CC_license) - CC Public Wiki - Shows how to place a license notice on a work. This is the step needed when a revised version is published.
3. [About CC Licenses](https://creativecommons.org/cc-licenses) - Creative Commons - Official overview of the six licenses, with plain-language summaries of the conditions each carries. The authoritative reference for what BY, SA, NC, and ND permit and require.
4. [Compatible Licenses](https://creativecommons.org/compatible-licenses) - Creative Commons - Explains which Creative Commons licenses may be combined in an adaptation and which license the result may carry. Direct support for checking whether two licenses allow a remix.
5. [License Chooser](https://creativecommons.org/chooser) - Creative Commons - An interactive questionnaire that leads a creator to a suitable license and produces the license statement. Good practice for the chapter's license selection exercise.

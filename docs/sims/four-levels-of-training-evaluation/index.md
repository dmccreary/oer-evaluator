## Description

Most workshops stop at a happy survey, which measures only the first level. This staircase uses the model of Donald Kirkpatrick that Chapter 19 names: reaction, learning, behavior, and results. Each step asks its question and shows the chapter's evidence for a workshop of 20 teachers. All the numbers are illustrative, made up in the chapter to show the form, and the poster marks them that way. An arrow beneath shows that later levels take longer to collect. Without a comparison group a change may have other causes, and counts of reuse are weaker than evidence of learning.

This MicroSim is a **grid overlay**: the poster is a drawing made for this book (SVG), and each zone is a clickable rectangle over it.

| Zone | What it shows |
|---|---|
| 1. Reaction | Did participants find the day useful? Illustrative evidence for a workshop of 20 teachers. |
| 2. Learning | Did they gain the skills? Illustrative evidence for a workshop of 20 teachers. |
| 3. Behavior | Do they use the skills afterward? Illustrative evidence for a workshop of 20 teachers. |
| 4. Results | Did it change outcomes? Illustrative evidence for a workshop of 20 teachers. |

### Controls

- **Explore and Quiz Me buttons:** switch modes. Quiz Me starts a new run.
- **The zones:** hover over a zone or press it to read its summary and facts in the panel below the poster.
- **Quiz questions:** read the question, then press the zone that answers it. A wrong press lets you try again.
- **Try Again:** repeats the quiz after the last question.

### Good to Know

Quiz mode scores each question as you answer it. The MicroSim does not store your answers. The poster prints its own headings, so the sim adds no labels on top of it.

- Every number on the poster is illustrative and made up in the chapter to show the form. Replace them with your own in data.json for a real workshop.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner can **place** a piece of workshop evidence at the right level of training evaluation and **explain** why a happy survey is not enough. (Bloom level: Understand.)

### Grade Level

Adult learners: teachers (K-12 and college), instructional designers, librarians, and OER coordinators.

### Duration

5-10 minutes.

### Prerequisites

- Training evaluation from [Chapter 19](../../chapters/19-training-certifying-reviewers/index.md) and the cautions of Chapters 16 and 17.

### Activities

1. **Explore (3 min).** Press each step. Which level is the easiest to collect, and which is the hardest?
2. **Quiz (3 min).** Press **Quiz Me** and place four pieces of evidence on the right step.
3. **Plan (4 min).** For a workshop you run, write one piece of evidence you could collect for each level.

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer against the poster.

1. "17 of 20 teachers earned the certificate" belongs to which level? *(Learning.)*
2. "9 components were reused by another teacher" belongs to which level? *(Results.)*
3. Why is a count of reuse weaker than evidence of learning? *(Without a comparison group, reuse may have other causes.)*

### Connection to the OER Evaluator rubric

The staircase is a ready frame for a grant report, alongside the Grant Response and Grant-Period Timeline workshop pages.

## References

1. Evaluating Professional Development - Thomas R. Guskey - Corwin Press (2000) - Guskey's five levels (participants' reactions, learning, organization support, use of new knowledge, and student outcomes) test whether a workshop changed practice, not only whether people enjoyed it.
2. [Professional certification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_certification) - Wikipedia - Explains how certifications are awarded after training and assessment, and what makes them credible. Background for the chapter's certificate requirements and submission review.
3. [Open Badges](https://www.1edtech.org/standards/open-badges) - 1EdTech - The standard for verifiable digital credentials that can carry evidence of what the earner did. A model for a certificate that links to a learner's reviewed work.

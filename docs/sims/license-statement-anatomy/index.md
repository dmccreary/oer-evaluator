## Description

A complete license statement does more than show a row of icons. This MicroSim draws the bottom of a lesson web page: a picture with a caption strip above a footer bar. Seven numbered markers point at the parts of a complete license statement. The drawing contains no text, so every label comes from the data in the sim.

In **Explore** mode you hover or click a marker to read its label, description, and tip. In **Quiz me** mode you read a hint, such as "A row of four small round icons at the left of the footer", and click the marker that matches. You get two tries.

| Marker | Part |
|---|---|
| 1 | License badge icons |
| 2 | License name and version |
| 3 | Link to the license |
| 4 | Copyright holder and year |
| 5 | Scope of the license |
| 6 | Notice for third-party content |
| 7 | Machine-readable license marking |

### Controls

- **Explore and Quiz me buttons:** switch modes. Quiz me starts a new run.
- **The seven numbered markers:** hover, focus with the Tab key, or click.
- **Next:** moves to the next hint.
- **Play again:** repeats the quiz as practice.

### Good to Know

Only your first committed answer on the first completed run counts as evidence of mastery. Practice runs give the same feedback but do not change that first-run score. Nothing moves until you act, and the MicroSim does not store your answers.

- A wrong first click says so and lets you try once more. A second wrong click reveals the marker. Only a correct first click counts as evidence.
- The drawing is made for this book (SVG) and carries no statistics.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective

After using this MicroSim, the learner can **identify** the seven parts of a complete license statement on an annotated page footer from a description of each part, with at least 6 of 7 correct on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Remember.)

### Grade Level

Adult learners: teachers (K-12 and college), instructional designers, librarians, and OER coordinators.

### Duration

5-10 minutes.

### Prerequisites

- The terms *license statement*, *license name and version*, and *third-party content* from [Chapter 2](../../chapters/02-open-licenses-attribution/index.md).

### Activities

1. **Explore (4 min).** Hover over every marker. Which part is invisible to a reader looking at the page?
2. **Quiz (4 min).** Press **Quiz me** and find all seven parts from their hints.
3. **Apply (3 min).** Open a real OER page and name which of the seven parts it shows and which it lacks.

### Assessment

Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer by using it.

1. Why is "CC BY" with no version an incomplete statement? *(Terms differ between versions, so the version must be named or linked.)*
2. Do the license icons alone make a license statement? *(No. They are a hint, and the written statement must be confirmed.)*
3. Does a license statement on a page cover every image on it? *(No. Treat each image as separately licensed until its caption says otherwise.)*
4. Where can a reader find the machine-readable marking? *(In the page source, or through a search tool's license filter.)*

### Connection to the OER Evaluator rubric

A complete license statement is what the open license metric looks for. [Chapter 9](../../chapters/09-modularity-metadata/index.md) scores how clearly a resource states what others may do.

# OER Evaluator

**Title:** OER Evaluator: Evaluating and Improving Open Educational Resources with AI Agents

**Target Audience:** Teachers (K-12 and college), instructional designers, librarians, and OER coordinators who select, adapt, or create open educational resources (OER)

**Prerequisites:** None. Readers should be comfortable using a web browser and a word processor. Experience with an AI chat assistant is helpful but not required; the book teaches the AI-agent skills it uses.

## Course Overview

Open educational resources (OER) are teaching and learning materials that carry an open license, so anyone can retain, reuse, revise, remix, and redistribute them. Finding OER is easy. Knowing whether a resource is good, and whether it can actually be reused in another course, is hard. A careful manual review of one textbook can take hours, and reviewers working from different checklists reach different conclusions.

This course teaches a repeatable method for judging an OER on 10 metrics worth 10 points each: five for **quality** and five for **reusability**. The quality metrics are accuracy and currency, alignment to learning outcomes, readability, accessibility, and practice with feedback. The reusability metrics are open license, modularity, metadata, editable source, and portability. Readers learn to score a resource by hand, then to direct an AI agent to make the first pass, check the agent's evidence, and decide which findings to accept, correct, or reject. Readers also learn the limits of agent scoring: invented citations, misread licenses, and overconfident scores.

The course then turns evaluation into improvement. Readers use AI agents to raise a resource's score by adding alt text, splitting a long chapter into reusable modules, writing metadata, generating practice questions, and building interactive simulations (MicroSims). Finally, readers learn to test whether a change helped. The main method is a manual A/B test with students, in which one group uses the original resource and another uses the improved version and the results are compared. Those results are then used to check and calibrate the automated scores, which is how AI skills for automated assessment of OER textbooks are validated.

## Main Topics Covered

- What OER are, the 5R permissions, and how OER differ from free or public-domain content
- Open licenses: the Creative Commons family, license compatibility, and attribution
- Why OER quality varies and what quality means for a learner
- The 10-metric rubric: scoring scales, evidence, and the 100-point total
- Quality metrics: accuracy and currency, alignment to learning outcomes, readability, accessibility, and practice with feedback
- Reusability metrics: open license, modularity, metadata, editable source, and portability
- Manual evaluation: scoring a resource by hand and recording evidence
- AI-agent evaluation: writing prompts and skills that score a resource and cite evidence
- Checking agent output: hallucinated citations, license misreads, and overconfident scores
- Calibrating agents against human reviewers and measuring agreement
- Improving a resource with AI agents, one metric at a time
- Designing reusable components: modular chapters, quizzes, glossaries, and MicroSims
- Metadata standards (such as Dublin Core) and open formats for editable source
- Accessibility standards (WCAG 2.1 AA) for text, images, video, and interactive content
- Manual A/B testing of an OER with students: design, sampling, and ethics
- Analyzing A/B test results and comparing them with rubric scores
- Publishing, sharing, and maintaining improved OER, including repositories and version control
- Training others and earning a certificate in OER quality evaluation with AI agents

## Topics Not Covered

- Writing a complete textbook from scratch or full instructional design for a course
- Copyright law beyond what is needed to read and apply open licenses
- Training or fine-tuning AI models; the course uses existing AI agents
- Programming beyond simple prompts, Markdown, and HTML needed to publish a resource
- Learning management system (LMS) administration and procurement of commercial materials
- Statistical methods beyond basic comparisons of two groups

## Learning Outcomes

After completing this course, students will be able to:

### Remember
*Retrieving, recognizing, and recalling relevant knowledge from long-term memory.*

- Define open educational resource and list the 5R permissions: retain, reuse, revise, remix, and redistribute
- Name the 10 metrics of the rubric and identify which five measure quality and which five measure reusability
- Recall the Creative Commons license elements (BY, SA, NC, ND) and the meaning of each
- List the required fields of a metadata record, such as title, author, subject, grade level, license, and description
- Identify common AI-agent errors, including invented citations, misread licenses, and overconfident scores
- State the purpose of an A/B test and name its two groups

### Understand
*Constructing meaning from instructional messages, including oral, written, and graphic communication.*

- Explain why a resource can be high quality but low in reusability, and the reverse, using examples
- Describe how each of the 10 metrics is scored and what evidence supports a score
- Explain how an AI agent scores a resource against a rubric and why a human must check its evidence
- Summarize how open licenses combine and why some combinations are incompatible
- Explain how modular content and editable source files make a resource easier to reuse
- Describe how accessibility features, such as alt text and heading structure, help learners

### Apply
*Carrying out or using a procedure in a given situation.*

- Score a sample OER on all 10 metrics by hand and record evidence for each score
- Write an AI-agent prompt that scores a resource against the rubric and returns evidence
- Add alt text, headings, and captions to a resource so it meets WCAG 2.1 AA
- Record complete metadata and a license statement for an existing resource
- Split a long chapter into modules that each stand alone and can be embedded elsewhere
- Run a small A/B test with two groups of students and record the outcomes

### Analyze
*Breaking material into constituent parts and determining how the parts relate to one another and to an overall structure or purpose.*

- Compare a teacher's scores with an agent's scores and find where they differ by more than 3 points
- Trace a low total score back to the specific metrics and passages that caused it
- Classify the errors in an agent's evaluation as factual, license, citation, or scoring errors
- Examine a resource's licenses and attributions to find conflicts that block reuse
- Compare A/B test results with rubric scores to find which metrics predict learner outcomes
- Differentiate improvements that raise the score from improvements that raise learning

### Evaluate
*Making judgments based on criteria and standards through checking and critiquing.*

- Judge whether an OER is ready to adopt, needs revision, or should be rejected, and justify the decision
- Critique an agent's evaluation and decide which findings to accept, correct, or reject
- Assess whether a revised resource gained on at least two quality and two reusability metrics
- Evaluate the reliability of the rubric by checking agreement between reviewers and agents
- Appraise the design of an A/B test for fairness, sample size, and student privacy
- Defend an adoption recommendation to a department or district using rubric evidence

### Create
*Putting elements together to form a coherent or functional whole; reorganizing elements into a new pattern or structure.*

- Design a reusable component, such as a quiz, glossary, or MicroSim, that other teachers can embed under an open license
- Build an AI-agent skill that evaluates OER automatically and reports scores with evidence
- Produce an improved version of an existing OER and publish it with metadata
- Design and run an A/B test that compares an original resource with its improved version
- Develop a training workshop that teaches colleagues to evaluate OER with AI agents
- **Capstone project:** evaluate one OER from your own subject, use AI agents to improve it on at least two quality and two reusability metrics, test the change with students, and publish the result under a CC BY license

# Story Cast and Style Sheet

This page is the source of truth for how recurring characters look and how image prompts are written in the OER Evaluator stories. Each image is generated by a separate request that remembers nothing about the other panels, so **every image prompt must repeat the full description of each character who appears in it**. Copy the text below word for word. Do not paraphrase it, and do not shorten it.

All characters are invented. Campuses are described by type, and no real person, organization, or brand appears in any image.

## House Style

Paste this sentence into every image prompt, then add the story's genre costume if it has one.

> Flat cartoon illustration, modern vector style, clean confident outlines, bold simple shapes, gentle humor, warm cream and soft gray neutrals with indigo (#3949ab) and burnt orange (#b45309) as the two accent colors, wide landscape 16:9 composition.

## Echo's Cameo

Echo appears somewhere in the background of **at least one panel** of every story, small enough that readers have to look for it. Name the panel and where Echo hides in that panel's prompt. Echo never speaks and never takes part in the action.

## Prompt Rules

1. **Start each prompt body** with the panel identifier line from the story template, and **end** it with the line "Generate the image immediately without asking clarifying questions."
2. **Repeat the house style sentence** and the full cast description of every named character in the panel.
3. **Give at least six specific visual details**: the setting, the props, the lighting, where each character stands and what they are doing, the facial expression, and one background gag.
4. **Keep text in the picture to a minimum.** Image generators garble words. Allow at most one or two short labels per image (for example a sign reading "OPEN" or a stamp reading "NOPE"), quote them exactly in the prompt, and put all dialogue in the narrative text under the panel.
5. **No brand names, logos, or real interface screenshots.** Draw generic stand-ins, such as "a generic laptop with a plain gray window". No real people.
6. **No violence, injuries, or anything alarming.** The stories are gentle comedies.
7. **Show competence.** Students and people who use assistive technology are always the sharpest people in the room, and are never the butt of a joke.
8. **Name the genre costume** when the story has one (noir, game show, storybook fable, and so on) in both the cover prompt and every panel prompt.

# References: Open Licenses and Attribution

1. [Creative Commons license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Commons_license) - Wikipedia - Describes the six main Creative Commons licenses, the BY, SA, NC, and ND conditions they combine, and the history of the license suite. Background for reading and comparing the licenses taught in this chapter.

2. [License compatibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/License_compatibility) - Wikipedia - Explains why works under different licenses cannot always be combined, with examples drawn mainly from open-source software. The same share-alike logic underlies this chapter's rules for combining openly licensed works.

3. [Fair use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_use) - Wikipedia - Covers the four-factor test in U.S. law, landmark cases, and the limits of the doctrine. Clarifies why fair use is a separate permission from an open license, which the chapter treats only briefly.

4. Copyright Law for Librarians and Educators: Creative Strategies and Practical Solutions (4th ed.) - Kenneth D. Crews - ALA Editions (2020) - Crews co-created the widely copied Fair Use Checklist, which turns the four statutory factors into a balance of reasons for and against. His book teaches teachers to reason through permissions step by step.

5. Made with Creative Commons - Paul Stacey and Sarah Hinchliff Pearson - Ctrl+Alt+Delete Books (2017) - Teaches licensing through case studies of creators and organizations that chose Creative Commons licenses, illustrated by Bryan Mathers. It shows how license choice follows from a creator's goals, as in this chapter's license selection section.

6. [About CC Licenses](https://creativecommons.org/cc-licenses) - Creative Commons - Official overview of the six licenses, with plain-language summaries of the conditions each carries. The authoritative reference for what BY, SA, NC, and ND permit and require.

7. [Recommended Practices for Attribution](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/wiki/Best_practices_for_attribution) - CC Public Wiki - Official guidance on crediting a CC-licensed work with its title, author, source, and license, with examples of good and weak credit lines. The model for the credit lines taught in the giving credit section.

8. [Compatible Licenses](https://creativecommons.org/compatible-licenses) - Creative Commons - Explains which Creative Commons licenses may be combined in an adaptation and which license the result may carry. Direct support for checking whether two licenses allow a remix.

9. [License Chooser](https://creativecommons.org/chooser) - Creative Commons - An interactive questionnaire that leads a creator to a suitable license and produces the license statement. Good practice for the chapter's license selection exercise.

10. [Measuring Fair Use: The Four Factors](https://fairuse.stanford.edu/overview/fair-use/four-factors/) - Stanford Copyright & Fair Use Center - Rich Stim's plain-English guide to the purpose, nature, amount, and market-effect factors, with examples from court decisions. Helps teachers see where fair use ends and open licenses begin.

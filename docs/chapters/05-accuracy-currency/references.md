# References: Accuracy and Currency

1. [Fact-checking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fact-checking) - Wikipedia - Describes how claims are checked before and after publication and the methods professional fact-checkers use. Supports this chapter's procedure for sampling and verifying the facts in a resource.

2. [Link rot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_rot) - Wikipedia - Explains how links stop working over time, how common the problem is, and what can be done about it. Background for the currency checks that test a resource's links.

3. [Peer review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review) - Wikipedia - Surveys how experts evaluate work before it is accepted or published, and the strengths and limits of the process. Informs the chapter's guidance on when a resource needs expert review.

4. Web Literacy for Student Fact-Checkers - Mike Caulfield - Pressbooks (2017) - Caulfield's "four moves" (check for previous work, go upstream to the source, read laterally, circle back) teach students to verify a claim by leaving the page. He later condensed them into the SIFT method.

5. Calling Bullshit: The Art of Skepticism in a Data-Driven World - Carl T. Bergstrom and Jevin D. West - Random House (2020) - Grew out of the authors' University of Washington course of the same name. Its examples of misleading statistics and charts give reviewers concrete questions to ask about any number in a resource.

6. [Civic Online Reasoning](https://cor.inquirygroup.org/) - Digital Inquiry Group - Free lessons and assessments built on lateral reading, the strategy professional fact-checkers use to judge a source. Gives teachers ready practice for checking an author's authority and a claim's support.

7. [SIFT (The Four Moves)](https://hapgood.us/2019/06/19/sift-the-four-moves/) - Hapgood - Mike Caulfield's own post explaining Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, and Trace claims. A short, usable routine for tracing a citation back to its origin.

8. [Evaluating Resources](https://guides.lib.berkeley.edu/evaluating-resources) - UC Berkeley Library - A library guide with questions about authority, purpose, currency, and bias for judging any source. Provides a ready checklist for the citation evaluation described in this chapter.

9. [Evaluating Sources of Information](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/research_and_citation/conducting_research/evaluating_sources_of_information/index.html) - Purdue OWL - Explains how to judge a source's author, publisher, date, and evidence. A plain companion for deciding whether a resource's citations are reliable and recent enough.

10. [Wayback Machine](https://web.archive.org/) - Internet Archive - Lets reviewers retrieve archived copies of pages that have moved or vanished and see what a page said on an earlier date. A practical tool for the broken-link and currency checks in this chapter.

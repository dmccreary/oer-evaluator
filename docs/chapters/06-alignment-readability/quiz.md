# Quiz: Alignment and Readability

Test your understanding of learning outcomes, Bloom's taxonomy, alignment checks, reading level, vocabulary, and plain language with these review questions.

---

#### 1. Which verb is measurable, meaning a teacher could observe a learner doing it?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Understand
2. Calculate
3. Appreciate
4. Be familiar with
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. A measurable verb names something a teacher could watch a learner do and check afterward. List, describe, calculate, compare, and design are measurable. Know, understand, learn, appreciate, and be familiar with are not, because no one can see them happen. A quick test is to ask, "How would I know the learner did this?" If the answer is "I couldn't tell," swap in a verb such as explain or label.

    **Concept Tested:** Measurable Verb

    **See:** [Measurable Verb](index.md#measurable-verb)

#### 2. Which three parts make up a well-formed learning objective in the model the chapter uses?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Topic, activity, and assessment
2. Outcome, standard, and sequence
3. Audience, verb, and grade level
4. Performance, condition, and criterion
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. The model comes from the educator Robert Mager. The performance is what the learner will do, written with a measurable verb. The condition is the situation or help the learner has, such as "given a map." The criterion is how well the learner must do it, such as "with no more than one error." An objective written this way can be assessed, unlike "Students will learn about runoff."

    **See:** [Learning Objective](index.md#learning-objective)

#### 3. Which list shows the six levels of the revised Bloom's taxonomy in order from the simplest to the most demanding?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Remember, understand, apply, evaluate, analyze, create
2. Understand, remember, apply, analyze, evaluate, create
3. Remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, create
4. Remember, apply, understand, analyze, create, evaluate
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. The 2001 revision by Anderson and Krathwohl orders the levels remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, and create, and each level asks more of the learner than the one before. The order matters to a reviewer because an outcome and the activities that teach it should sit at the same level. If an outcome says "predict" and every activity asks only for recall, learners never practice the skill the outcome names.

    **Concept Tested:** Bloom Cognitive Levels

    **See:** [Bloom Cognitive Levels](index.md#bloom-cognitive-levels)

#### 4. Why does the chapter say that a standard code printed beside an activity is not evidence of alignment?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The code states what the author hopes, so a reviewer must read the standard and check what learners do
2. Standard codes are usually out of date by the time a resource is published and reviewed
3. Only state standards can be matched to activities, and codes come from national bodies
4. A code shows that the activity matches the standard, but at the Remember level only
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Listing a code is not matching. The reviewer reads the standard, finds the verb it asks for, and looks for an activity that asks learners to do that. A strong match has learners doing what the standard names, a partial match touches the topic without the performance, and no match shares only a topic word. For example, coloring a picture of a river does not meet a standard that asks learners to explain how water moves.

    **Concept Tested:** Standards Alignment

    **See:** [Standards Alignment](index.md#standards-alignment)

#### 5. An outcome says "Explain how water moves over land," but every quiz item asks learners to choose a vocabulary word. What is the problem?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The outcome uses a vague verb such as 'know', so no quiz item could check it
2. The items check a different topic from the one that the outcome names
3. The items check the right topic at a lower level than the outcome asks
4. The outcome has no activity, so nothing in the resource teaches it to learners
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. An assessment item is aligned when it checks the outcome at the level the outcome asks. Here the topic matches, but "explain" asks for understanding and the items ask only for recall, so the level is wrong. This failure is easy to miss because the topic lines up. The fix is to raise the level of the questions. "Explain" is a measurable verb (A), and the problem lies in the items, not the teaching (D).

    **Concept Tested:** Assessment Alignment

    **See:** [Assessment Alignment](index.md#assessment-alignment)

#### 6. Why can a passage score as easy on the Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level and still be hard to read?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The formula works only on passages of more than 1,000 words, and short samples give no result
2. The formula measures vocabulary tiers and ignores sentence length, so it misses long sentences
3. The formula gives a score out of 10 and not a school grade, so it cannot show reading level
4. The formula counts sentence and word length, so it cannot see dense ideas, undefined terms, or poor organization
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. The formula uses three counts: sentences, words, and syllables. It measures the surface of the text. A passage of short words and short sentences can score as easy and still be hard if its ideas are dense or its terms are never defined. Treat the number as one piece of evidence and read the passage yourself. For a stable result, use passages of at least 100 words, not 1,000.

    **Concept Tested:** Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level

    **See:** [Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level](index.md#flesch-kincaid-grade-level)

#### 7. A passage has 5 sentences, 100 words, and 150 syllables. Using grade = 0.39 × (words ÷ sentences) + 11.8 × (syllables ÷ words) − 15.59, what is its Flesch-Kincaid grade level?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. 7.8
2. 9.9
3. 13.0
4. 25.5
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Words per sentence is 100 divided by 5, which is 20, and 0.39 times 20 is 7.8. Syllables per word is 150 divided by 100, which is 1.5, and 11.8 times 1.5 is 17.7. Adding gives 25.5, and subtracting 15.59 gives 9.91, which rounds to 9.9. Option A stops after the sentence part, and option D forgets the final subtraction that lines the result up with school grades.

    **Concept Tested:** Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level

    **See:** [Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level](index.md#flesch-kincaid-grade-level)

#### 8. Which rewrite of "Utilize the glossary for the purpose of locating unfamiliar terms" is plainest while keeping the meaning?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Look at the glossary
2. Utilize the glossary to locate terms
3. Use of the glossary is advised for the location of unfamiliar terms
4. Use the glossary to find words you do not know
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Plain language uses short sentences with one idea, the active voice, and common words where a common word is as accurate. Option D keeps both the action and the purpose in everyday words. Option A is short but loses the purpose, option B keeps the stiff word "utilize," and option C buries the action in a passive, noun-heavy phrase. Plain language is not dumbing down, because the ideas stay the same and the clutter goes away.

    **Concept Tested:** Plain Language

    **See:** [Plain Language](index.md#plain-language)

#### 9. A water unit has four sections. Section 1 introduces watershed and tributary. Section 2 introduces runoff and uses watershed. Section 3 introduces flood and uses runoff and infiltration. Section 4 introduces infiltration and uses runoff. What is the sequence problem, and how is it fixed?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Section 3 uses infiltration before section 4 introduces it, so section 4 should move up before section 3
2. Section 2 uses watershed before it is introduced, so section 2 should come before section 1
3. Section 4 uses runoff before it is introduced, so section 4 should come before section 2
4. There is no problem, because every idea is introduced somewhere in the unit before the quiz
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. To check sequence, list what each section introduces and what it uses, then look for a section that uses an idea that arrives later. Section 3 uses infiltration, but section 4 is where infiltration is introduced, so learners meet the idea before they are taught it. Moving section 4 ahead of section 3 fixes it. Option D is the misconception that an idea appearing anywhere in the unit is enough.

    **Concept Tested:** Scope and Sequence

    **See:** [Scope and Sequence](index.md#scope-and-sequence)

#### 10. In a curriculum map for a fraction unit, the outcome "Add two fractions with the same denominator" is taught by Section 3, but no quiz item checks it. How should a reviewer classify and fix this?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. A content gap in teaching, so add a new section that teaches the outcome to learners
2. Content with no outcome, so delete Section 3 from the unit to shorten the resource
3. A content gap in checking, so add a quiz item that checks the outcome at the level it asks
4. A level mismatch in teaching, so raise the Bloom level of Section 3
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. A content gap is an outcome that is not taught, not assessed, or not assessed at the level it asks. The gap can lie in the teaching, in the checking, or in both, and the fix differs: add an activity, add a question, or raise the level of the question. Here the outcome is taught but never checked, so the fix is a quiz item. The reverse problem, content with no outcome, is noted but is not a gap.

    **Concept Tested:** Content Gap

    **See:** [Content Gap](index.md#content-gap)

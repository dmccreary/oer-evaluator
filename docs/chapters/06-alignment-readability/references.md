# References: Alignment and Readability

1. [Bloom's taxonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloom%27s_taxonomy) - Wikipedia - Describes the original and revised Bloom's taxonomies and their six cognitive levels with example verbs. Supports the chapter's method of matching a resource's activities to the level of its stated outcomes.

2. [Flesch–Kincaid readability tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flesch%E2%80%93Kincaid_readability_tests) - Wikipedia - Gives the formulas for the Reading Ease and Grade Level tests, their history, and their limits. Background for the reading level checks in the readability metric.

3. [Constructive alignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructive_alignment) - Wikipedia - Explains John Biggs's principle that outcomes, teaching activities, and assessment should match one another. The idea behind this chapter's alignment metric and its outcome-to-content mapping.

4. Teaching for Quality Learning at University (4th ed.) - John Biggs and Catherine Tang - McGraw-Hill / Open University Press (2011) - Biggs introduced constructive alignment, asking teachers to write outcomes as verbs and then design activities and assessments that require those verbs. It is the basis of this chapter's outcome-to-content mapping.

5. Understanding by Design (2nd expanded ed.) - Grant Wiggins and Jay McTighe - ASCD (2005) - Wiggins and McTighe's backward design starts with desired results, then evidence, then activities. A reviewer can follow the same sequence to judge whether a resource's content serves its stated outcomes.

6. [Plain Language Guide Series](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language) - Digital.gov - Federal guidance on writing short sentences, active voice, and familiar words, with examples. Gives concrete criteria for judging a resource's plain-language quality and text structure.

7. [Learning Objectives](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/designteach/design/learningobjectives.html) - Carnegie Mellon Eberly Center - Explains how to write measurable learning objectives with action verbs and how objectives shape assessment and activities. Helps reviewers judge whether a resource's stated objectives are testable.

8. [Common Core State Standards Initiative](https://corestandards.org/) - CCSSI - Home of the K-12 English language arts and mathematics standards. Reviewers scoring a K-12 resource use these standards as the target when checking alignment.

9. [Readability Formulas](https://readabilityformulas.com/) - Readability Formulas - Explains common readability formulas and offers a free online test for pasted text. Lets reviewers check a passage's reading level quickly and see how the formulas differ.

10. [Legibility, Readability, and Comprehension: Making Users Read Your Words](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/legibility-readability-comprehension/) - Nielsen Norman Group - Separates how legible the type is, how readable the words are, and how well readers understand. A reminder that a reading-level score alone does not measure comprehension.

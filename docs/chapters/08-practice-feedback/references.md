# References: Practice and Feedback

1. [Formative assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formative_assessment) - Wikipedia - Describes assessment used during learning to guide teaching and give feedback, as opposed to grading at the end. Background for judging whether a resource's questions help learners improve.

2. [Testing effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testing_effect) - Wikipedia - Summarizes research showing that retrieving information from memory strengthens it more than rereading. Supports the chapter's sections on quizzes and retrieval practice.

3. [Worked-example effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worked-example_effect) - Wikipedia - Explains how studying fully worked solutions helps novices learn faster than solving problems alone, within cognitive load theory. Background for scoring whether a resource includes worked examples.

4. Make It Stick: The Science of Successful Learning - Peter C. Brown, Henry L. Roediger III, and Mark A. McDaniel - Harvard University Press (2014) - Turns laboratory research on retrieval practice, spacing, and interleaving into stories of real learners, arguing that effortful recall beats rereading. A clear base for judging a resource's practice design.

5. Embedded Formative Assessment (2nd ed.) - Dylan Wiliam - Solution Tree Press (2018) - Wiliam's five key strategies (clarify intentions, elicit evidence, give feedback that moves learners forward, activate peers, activate ownership) give reviewers a practical test of whether questions and feedback do formative work.

6. [RetrievalPractice.org](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/) - Pooja K. Agarwal - Cognitive scientist Pooja Agarwal's free guides, strategies, and research summaries on retrieval practice and spacing. Gives teachers ready examples of the practice this chapter asks reviewers to look for.

7. [The Learning Scientists](https://www.learningscientists.org/) - The Learning Scientists - Cognitive psychologists explain six research-based study strategies, including spaced practice and retrieval practice, with downloadable materials. Useful for judging whether a resource lets learners use them.

8. [Formative vs Summative Assessment](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/assessment/basics/formative-summative.html) - Carnegie Mellon Eberly Center - Contrasts assessment that guides learning with assessment that measures it, with examples of each. Helps reviewers decide whether a resource's quizzes give feedback or only a grade.

9. [PhET Interactive Simulations](https://phet.colorado.edu/) - University of Colorado Boulder - Free, research-based simulations in science and mathematics that let learners change variables and see results. A model of the interactive simulations the chapter says earn their place.

10. [Creating Exams](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/assessment/assesslearning/creatingexams.html) - Carnegie Mellon Eberly Center - Guidance on designing exams and writing different question types, with tips for tying items to learning objectives. Supports the chapter's criteria for judging question quality.

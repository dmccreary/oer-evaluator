# Chapters

This textbook is organized into 19 chapters covering 351 concepts.

## Chapter Overview

1. [Foundations of Open Educational Resources](01-foundations-of-oer/index.md) - Defines open educational resources and the 5R permissions, and explains adoption, adaptation, and creation, giving teachers the shared vocabulary needed to select and judge open materials.
2. [Open Licenses and Attribution](02-open-licenses-attribution/index.md) - Explains the Creative Commons license family, its elements, attribution, and compatibility, so teachers can read a license, give correct credit, and choose a license for their own work.
3. [The Ten-Metric Rubric and Scoring](03-ten-metric-rubric-scoring/index.md) - Introduces the rubric that scores an OER on five quality and five reusability metrics, including score scales, evidence, subtotals, reviewers, and what makes a rubric reliable and valid.
4. [Open File Formats and Version Control](04-open-formats-version-control/index.md) - Covers the editable source metric: open file formats, Markdown and HTML, and version control with Git and GitHub, which make a resource easy to revise and remix.
5. [Accuracy and Currency](05-accuracy-currency/index.md) - Teaches teachers to score accuracy and currency by fact checking, evaluating citations, spotting outdated content and broken links, and deciding when expert review is needed.
6. [Alignment and Readability](06-alignment-readability/index.md) - Shows how to score alignment to learning outcomes, standards, and Bloom's levels, and readability through reading level, vocabulary, plain language, and how well the text fits its audience.
7. [Accessibility](07-accessibility/index.md) - Explains how to score accessibility against WCAG 2.1 AA, covering alt text, headings, color contrast, captions, keyboard use, screen readers, accessible math, and automated checkers.
8. [Practice and Feedback](08-practice-feedback/index.md) - Covers the practice metric: worked examples, formative assessment, quizzes, question quality, immediate feedback, retrieval practice, and interactive simulations that let learners try and check their understanding.
9. [Modularity and Metadata](09-modularity-metadata/index.md) - Shows how to score the open license, modularity, and metadata metrics, including standalone components, Dublin Core records, controlled vocabularies, and machine-readable metadata that help others find and reuse a resource.
10. [Portability and Reuse Across Platforms](10-portability-reuse/index.md) - Explains how to score portability: browser compatibility, no-login access, iframe embedding, LMS import, Common Cartridge, and the platform lock-in that stops a resource from moving.
11. [Manual Evaluation and Adoption Decisions](11-manual-evaluation-adoption/index.md) - Walks through scoring an OER by hand, recording evidence, calibrating and reaching consensus with other reviewers, and turning the total score into an adopt, revise, or reject decision.
12. [AI Agents, Prompts, and Rubric Scoring](12-ai-agents-prompts-scoring/index.md) - Introduces AI assistants, language models, agents, skills, and prompts, and shows how to build a rubric prompt that makes an agent score a resource and return its evidence.
13. [Automated Checks, Cost, and Student Privacy](13-automated-checks-cost-privacy/index.md) - Covers multi-agent review, keeping a human in the loop, automated link, accessibility, license, and readability checks, AI cost, and protecting student data under FERPA.
14. [Checking and Calibrating AI Agent Output](14-checking-agent-output/index.md) - Teaches teachers to catch hallucinations, invented citations, license misreadings, and overconfident scores, and to measure agreement with human reviewers using simple statistics.
15. [Improving a Resource with AI Agents](15-improving-resources-with-ai/index.md) - Guides teachers through an improvement plan that uses agents to fix alt text, headings, reading level, metadata, and links, then re-scores the resource and reviews every change.
16. [Designing an A/B Test with Students](16-ab-testing-design/index.md) - Explains how to compare an original and an improved resource with students, covering hypotheses, control and treatment groups, before-and-after comparison, pre-tests and post-tests, sampling, consent, and student privacy.
17. [Analyzing A/B Test Results](17-ab-testing-analysis/index.md) - Shows how to collect and anonymize data, compare group means, read effect size, combine surveys with qualitative feedback, check rubric scores against outcomes, and consider equity.
18. [Reusable Components and Publishing OER](18-components-publishing-oer/index.md) - Shows how to package components, build a library, publish improved OER with MkDocs and GitHub Pages, share through a clearinghouse, track reuse, and report impact.
19. [Training Teachers and Certifying Reviewers](19-training-certifying-reviewers/index.md) - Describes how to run a teacher training workshop, set certificate requirements, review submissions, train local reviewers, and finish with a capstone project that improves a real OER.

## How to Use This Textbook

Read the chapters in order. Each chapter builds on concepts from the earlier ones, and the dependencies between concepts are respected so that a concept is always introduced after its prerequisites. If you are already comfortable with a topic, check a chapter's list of concepts and its prerequisites, then skip ahead.

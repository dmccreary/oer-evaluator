# References: Accessibility

1. [Web Content Accessibility Guidelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_Content_Accessibility_Guidelines) - Wikipedia - Explains the WCAG versions, their four principles, and the A, AA, and AAA conformance levels. The standard behind this book's accessibility metric, which targets WCAG 2.1 AA.

2. [Web accessibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_accessibility) - Wikipedia - Surveys barriers that disabilities create on the web, the laws that require access, and common solutions. Background for why alt text, headings, captions, and keyboard use matter to learners.

3. [Screen reader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Screen_reader) - Wikipedia - Describes how screen readers turn on-screen content into speech or braille and what they need from a page's structure. Helps reviewers understand why headings and alt text are scored.

4. A Web for Everyone: Designing Accessible User Experiences - Sarah Horton and Whitney Quesenbery - Rosenfeld Media (2014) - The authors frame accessibility around people rather than checklists, using personas such as a person with low vision or a person using speech input to show why each guideline exists.

5. Universal Design for Learning: Theory and Practice - Anne Meyer, David H. Rose, and David Gordon - CAST Professional Publishing (2014) - The authors argue for designing for learner variability from the start, with multiple means of representation such as captions and text alternatives. Explains why accessibility features help every learner.

6. [WCAG 2 Overview](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/) - W3C Web Accessibility Initiative - The W3C's overview of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines covering versions 2.0, 2.1, and 2.2, with links to the full standard and quick references. The official starting point for the accessibility metric.

7. [Alternative Text](https://webaim.org/techniques/alttext/) - WebAIM - Explains when and how to write alt text for informative, decorative, functional, and complex images, with examples. Directly supports the chapter's section on images.

8. [Contrast Checker](https://webaim.org/resources/contrastchecker/) - WebAIM - A free tool that takes a foreground and background color and reports the contrast ratio against the WCAG AA and AAA thresholds. Lets reviewers measure color contrast instead of guessing.

9. [Keyboard Accessibility](https://webaim.org/techniques/keyboard/) - WebAIM - Explains how people use a page without a mouse, including tab order, visible focus, and skip links. Gives a simple procedure for the chapter's keyboard test.

10. [WAVE Web Accessibility Evaluation Tools](https://wave.webaim.org/) - WebAIM - A free tool that marks errors and alerts directly on a web page. Shows which accessibility problems automated checks can find and which still need a human reviewer.

11. [The 6th "R" of Intelligent Textbooks](https://dmccreary.github.io/intelligent-textbooks/appendices/five-rs-of-intelligent-textbooks/) - Dan McCreary, Intelligent Textbooks - Extends David Wiley's 5Rs of open content (retain, reuse, revise, remix, redistribute) with a sixth right, regenerate, for rebuilding a textbook from its learning graph and course description. The rights to revise and remix are what let someone other than the author fix a missing alt text or caption.

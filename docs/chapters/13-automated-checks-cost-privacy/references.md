# References: Automated Checks, Cost, and Student Privacy

1. [Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Educational_Rights_and_Privacy_Act) - Wikipedia - Explains the U.S. law that protects the privacy of student education records and when schools may disclose them. The legal basis for the chapter's student data privacy guidance.

2. [Human-in-the-loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human-in-the-loop) - Wikipedia - Describes systems in which a person reviews or corrects an automated process. Background for the chapter's rule that a human checks the agent's evidence before any score is accepted.

3. [Multi-agent system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-agent_system) - Wikipedia - Surveys systems where several agents work together or check one another. Supports the chapter's multi-agent review, in which one agent's findings are triaged against another's.

4. Privacy in Context: Technology, Policy, and the Integrity of Social Life - Helen Nissenbaum - Stanford Law Books (2010) - Nissenbaum's contextual integrity judges a flow of information by whether it matches the norms of the setting where it was shared. It offers a test for whether student work may be sent to an AI service.

5. Co-Intelligence: Living and Working with AI - Ethan Mollick - Portfolio (2024) - Mollick's practical rules, including always being the human in the loop, and his centaur and cyborg modes of working with AI frame this chapter's balance between automation and human judgment.

6. [Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA)](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/ferpa) - U.S. Department of Education - The Student Privacy Policy Office's official FERPA page, with guidance for schools and teachers on education records and disclosure. The primary source for the chapter's privacy rules.

7. [Common Sense Privacy Program](https://privacy.commonsense.org/) - Common Sense Media - Evaluates the privacy policies of educational apps and tools and rates them. Helps teachers check an AI service's data practices before sending it any student material.

8. [Web Accessibility Evaluation Tools List](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/tools/list/) - W3C Web Accessibility Initiative - A filterable directory of accessibility checkers by purpose, platform, price, and standard. Helps readers choose the automated accessibility checks this chapter recommends.

9. [Pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing) - Claude Platform Docs - Lists per-token prices and discounts for batch processing and prompt caching. Gives the numbers needed for the chapter's exercise on estimating the cost of scoring a batch of resources.

10. [AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) - NIST - A voluntary framework for governing, mapping, measuring, and managing the risks of AI systems. Supports decisions about which checks to automate and which need human oversight.

# References: Analyzing A/B Test Results

1. [Effect size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effect_size) - Wikipedia - Explains effect sizes such as Cohen's d, how they are computed, and how to interpret them. Supports the chapter's section on reading the size of a difference, not only whether it exists.

2. [Student's t-test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student%27s_t-test) - Wikipedia - Describes the t-test for comparing two group means, its assumptions, and how to read the result. Background for the chapter's comparison of mean scores between groups.

3. [Pearson correlation coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_correlation_coefficient) - Wikipedia - Defines the correlation coefficient and how to interpret its sign and size. Supports the chapter's check of whether rubric scores track learning outcomes.

4. Statistical Power Analysis for the Behavioral Sciences (2nd ed.) - Jacob Cohen - Lawrence Erlbaum Associates (1988) - Cohen introduced the standardized difference d and offered 0.2, 0.5, and 0.8 as rough small, medium, and large conventions. He also showed how to plan sample size for them. The source of this chapter's effect size convention.

5. Understanding the New Statistics: Effect Sizes, Confidence Intervals, and Meta-Analysis - Geoff Cumming - Routledge (2012) - Cumming's estimation approach asks how big the effect is and how uncertain, instead of only whether p is below .05. His "dance of the p values" shows how much results vary between samples.

6. [Using Effect Size—or Why the P Value Is Not Enough](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3444174/) - Journal of Graduate Medical Education - Sullivan and Feinn explain why a significant p value says little about how large or useful a difference is. A short, readable argument for reporting effect size.

7. [Interpreting Cohen's d](https://rpsychologist.com/cohend/) - R Psychologist (Kristoffer Magnusson) - An interactive visualization showing how two overlapping distributions change as Cohen's d grows. Helps teachers picture what a small or medium effect looks like.

8. [Online Statistics Education](https://onlinestatbook.com/2/index.html) - Rice University and partners - A free introductory statistics text covering means, t-tests, correlation, and confidence intervals with simulations. Enough background for the basic two-group comparisons in this chapter.

9. [Writing Survey Questions](https://www.pewresearch.org/writing-survey-questions) - Pew Research Center - Practical guidance on wording survey questions and answer choices to avoid bias and confusion. Supports the chapter's design of a student survey.

10. [UDL Guidelines](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/) - CAST - The Universal Design for Learning guidelines describe how to give learners varied ways to engage, understand, and show what they know. A lens for the chapter's equity consideration.

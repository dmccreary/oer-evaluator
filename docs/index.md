**Evaluating and Improving Open Educational Resources with AI Agents**

Finding open educational resources (OER) is easy. Knowing whether one is good,
and whether you can reuse it in your own course, is hard. A careful review of
a single textbook can take hours, and reviewers working from different
checklists reach different conclusions.

This book teaches a repeatable method. You score a resource on **10 metrics
worth 10 points each**, five for quality and five for reusability, for a total
of 100 points. The result is the **OpenBook QR Score**, the total with its quality (Q) and reusability (R) halves, such as QR 65 (Q 32 / R 33). You learn to score by hand, direct an AI agent to make the first
pass, check the agent's evidence, improve the resource, and test whether the
change helped with real students.

## The Ten Metrics

| Quality (50 points) | Reusability (50 points) |
|---------------------|-------------------------|
| Accuracy and currency | Open license |
| Alignment to learning outcomes | Modularity |
| Readability | Metadata |
| Accessibility | Editable source |
| Practice with feedback | Portability |

## How the Method Works

1. **Score by hand.** Read a resource, score each metric, and record the
   evidence behind every score.
2. **Direct an AI agent.** Write a prompt that makes an agent score the same
   resource and cite its evidence.
3. **Check the agent.** Catch invented citations, misread licenses, and
   overconfident scores, and measure how closely the agent agrees with human
   reviewers.
4. **Improve and test.** Use agents to raise the lowest metrics, then run a
   manual A/B test in which one group of students uses the original and
   another uses the improved version.

## Who This Book Is For

Teachers (K-12 and college), instructional designers, librarians, and OER
coordinators who select, adapt, or create open educational resources. There
are no prerequisites. You should be comfortable with a web browser and a word
processor. Experience with an AI chat assistant helps but is not required; the
book teaches the agent skills it uses.

## Learn by Doing

- **[MicroSims](sims/index.md)**: 51 interactive simulations, 30 of them
  overlay posters. Match scenarios to the 5R permissions, move sliders to score
  a sample resource and see the adopt, revise, or reject decision, or compare
  two reviewers' scores.
- **[Posters](posters/index.md)**: 31 reference posters, one or more for each
  stage of the book. Hover or press to explore each one, then take its quiz.
- **[Stories](stories/index.md)**: six short, illustrated graphic-novel stories
  that help you remember how to evaluate OER, with and without AI agents.
- **Chapter quizzes**: 190 questions across 19 chapters, with an annotated
  reference list for every chapter.
- **[Glossary](glossary.md)**: 351 defined terms.
- **[FAQ](faq.md)**: 100 questions and answers.
- **[Learning Graph](learning-graph/index.md)**: how the 351 concepts depend on
  each other, so each one appears after its prerequisites.

## Meet Echo

Echo the Loon is the book's guide. A loon dives below the surface to see what
is really there, which is what you do when you check evidence and verify what
an AI agent reports. [Meet Echo](img/mascot/character-sheet.md).

## For Workshop Leaders

The [workshop materials](workshops/index.md) include a sample one-day training
schedule for about 20 teachers and a fill-in rubric worksheet. The
[Rubric Specification](rubric-spec.md) describes the scoring design in detail;
it is a draft that has not yet been validated against student results.

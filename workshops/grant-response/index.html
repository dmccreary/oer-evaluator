    Grant Response: AI-Assisted OER Evaluation Training for Teachers¶

    Summary¶

    We request $1,000 from the MNOP OER Pathfinder Grant to run a one-day, in-person training in Minneapolis for 20 K-12 and college teachers. Teachers learn to score open educational resources (OER) on 10 quality and reusability metrics from the OER Evaluator textbook, using AI agents to do the first pass. They then use AI agents to improve one existing OER and publish it as a reusable, openly licensed component. Teachers who finish earn a Certificate of OER Quality Evaluation Using AI Agents.

    The sections below follow the proposal form field by field.

    3. Institutional Context¶

    The project builds on the OER Evaluator textbook, an open textbook in development (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) hosted on GitHub. This interactive intelligent textbook are built with the same AI-agent workflow the training teaches: agents draft content, and automated checks score each component. Existing checks score interactive simulations (MicroSims) on a 100-point rubric and test glossary definitions against the ISO 11179 standard for precise, non-circular definitions. The key asset is a highly detailed agent skill that is use to evaluate a textbook. See OER Evaluator Skill.

    4. Project Rationale¶

    Teachers can find OER for almost any course, but they lack a fast, shared way to judge its quality or tell whether it can really be reused. A careful manual review of one textbook can take hours, and reviewers using different checklists reach different conclusions. Many OER are also hard to reuse: they ship as one PDF, carry unclear licenses, or bundle content that cannot be lifted into another course.

    AI agents change the cost of review. An agent can check a chapter against a rubric in minutes for missing alt text, reading level, broken links, license terms, and alignment to learning outcomes. The same agents can fix much of what they find. But agents also make confident mistakes, such as invented citations or misread licenses, so teachers need training to direct agents, check their output, and decide what to keep.

    Why now: AI tools are widely available to teachers, yet OER review is still mostly manual and ad hoc. One day of training on a common rubric gives a cohort of teachers a shared method, a credential that signals it, and a set of improved OER components that any teacher in the region can reuse. Each certified teacher can then lead the same review in their own school or department.

    5. Project Plan¶

    Proposed activities

    1. Build and pilot (November 2026 to January 2027). Publish the 10-metric rubric in the OER Evaluator textbook: five quality metrics (accuracy, alignment to learning outcomes, readability, accessibility, practice with feedback) and five reusability metrics (open license, modularity, metadata, editable source, portability). Package AI-agent prompts that score an OER against the rubric and cite evidence for each score. Pilot both with two or three teachers, and recruit 20 participants through the applicant institution and MNOP networks.
    2. Training day (Saturday, February 20, 2027, Minneapolis). Teachers score a sample OER by hand, compare their scores with the agent's, and learn where agents err. In the afternoon improvement challenge, each teacher uses AI agents to raise the score of one component of an OER they already use: adding alt text, splitting a chapter into reusable modules, writing metadata, generating practice questions, or building an interactive simulation. Teachers re-score their work and publish it under a CC BY license.
    3. Certificate and follow-up (February to April 2027). Within 30 days, teachers submit an evaluation of one OER from their own subject, with a human check of every agent finding, and an improved component that gains on at least two quality and two reusability metrics. Completers receive the Certificate of OER Quality Evaluation Using AI Agents. Volunteers compare the original and improved versions with their students in a simple A/B test.
    4. Package and share (May 2027). Publish the training kit and findings to the MNOP Clearinghouse, submit the final report, and present at the wrap-up event.

    Deliverables

    1. An open training kit (CC BY 4.0): rubric, agent prompts, slides, facilitator guide, and certificate requirements, ready for any school to rerun.
    2. At least 10 teacher-improved OER components with before-and-after scores, plus a short findings report on how often agent scores matched teacher scores.

    Anticipated impact

    • 20 teachers trained; at least 15 earn the certificate.
    • Improved components gain an average of 15 or more points on the 100-point rubric.
    • At least 80% of participants report confidence evaluating OER with AI agents on the exit survey.
    • At least 5 teachers adopt or adapt an OER in a spring 2027 course.
    • Certified teachers become local reviewers who repeat the process in their schools.

    Use of funds ($1,000)

    • $500: completion stipends of $50 for up to 10 teachers who publish an improved component
    • $300: lunch and refreshments for about 24 participants and facilitators
    • $120: AI usage credits for the shared evaluation agent on training day
    • $80: printed certificates, rubric cards, and supplies

    The applicant institution provides the venue in kind, and the facilitators donate their time. If no institution can be found to host for free we may rent a training room.

    6. Previous or Related Work¶

    The closest related work is the OER Evaluator textbook and the AI-agent toolchain behind it, which already generates open textbook components and scores them automatically. This grant adds what the toolchain cannot supply on its own: teachers trained to run these agents, check their output, and apply the method to OER they already use.

    7. Commitment Statement¶

    Our team will attend the virtual kickoff and the check-ins about every two months. We will submit a one-to-two-page final report by May 28, 2027, share the training kit and improved OER through the MNOP Clearinghouse, and present at the regional wrap-up event in late May or early June 2027.

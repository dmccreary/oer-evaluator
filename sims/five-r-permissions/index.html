    The 5R Permissions: Five Things You May Do¶

    Run the 5R Permissions: Five Things You May Do MicroSim Fullscreen
    Chapter 1: Foundations of Open Educational Resources

    To change the content, edit data.json in this folder. The artwork is five-r-permissions.svg, and scripts/posters/specs/ holds the code that draws it.

    You can add this MicroSim to any web page with this iframe:

    <iframe src="https://dmccreary.github.io/oer-evaluator/sims/five-r-permissions/main.html"
        height="741px"
        width="100%"
        scrolling="no"></iframe>

    Description¶

    This poster shows the five permissions that an open license grants, in the order Chapter 1 teaches them: retain, reuse, revise, remix, and redistribute. Every panel has the same structure: a scene, a plain-language meaning, the test question to ask of any resource, and one classroom example. A strip under the panels shows that the permissions build on one another.

    This MicroSim is a grid overlay: the poster is a drawing made for this book (SVG), and each zone is a clickable rectangle over it.

    Zone What it shows
    Retain Make, own, and control copies of the resource.
    Reuse Use the resource in its original, unaltered form.
    Revise Adapt, adjust, modify, or alter the resource itself.
    Remix Combine the work with other open material to create something new.
    Redistribute Share copies of the original, revised, or remixed work with others.

    Controls¶

    • Explore and Quiz Me buttons: switch modes. Quiz Me starts a new run.
    • The zones: hover over a zone or press it to read its summary and facts in the panel below the poster.
    • Quiz questions: read the question, then press the zone that answers it. A wrong press lets you try again.
    • Try Again: repeats the quiz after the last question.

    Good to Know¶

    Quiz mode scores each question as you answer it. The MicroSim does not store your answers. The poster prints its own headings, so the sim adds no labels on top of it.

    • The five panels run in sequence, not in rank. No permission is more important than another.

    Lesson Plan¶

    Learning Objective¶

    After using this MicroSim, the learner can identify which of the 5R permissions a classroom action uses, with at least 4 of 5 correct on the first attempt. (Bloom level: Remember.)

    Grade Level¶

    Adult learners: teachers (K-12 and college), instructional designers, librarians, and OER coordinators.

    Duration¶

    5-10 minutes.

    Prerequisites¶

    • The meaning of an open license from Chapter 1.

    Activities¶

    1. Explore (3 min). Press each panel. Which two permissions are easiest to confuse, and what separates them?
    2. Quiz (3 min). Press Quiz Me and match five classroom actions to their permissions.
    3. Practice (4 min). Write one action of your own for each permission. Swap with a partner and check each other.

    Assessment¶

    Ask learners to answer without the MicroSim, then check each answer against the poster.

    1. You fix a typo in an open worksheet. Which permission is that? (Revise. Reuse stops where change begins.)
    2. You email an unchanged open worksheet to a colleague. Which permission? (Redistribute.)
    3. Which permission lets you build one unit from chapters of two open textbooks? (Remix, and the licenses must be compatible.)

    Connection to the OER Evaluator rubric¶

    Revise leads to the editable source metric, remix leads to license compatibility, and redistribute leads to ShareAlike. The 5Rs are the vocabulary of every later chapter.

    References¶

    1. Definition of Open - opencontent.org - David Wiley's own statement of the 5R permissions (retain, reuse, revise, remix, redistribute) and why a license must grant all five. This is the source of the 5R framework used throughout the book.
    2. Open educational resources - Wikipedia - Defines OER, traces the movement's history, and lists the 5R permissions with examples of repositories and national policies. Anchors this chapter's definition of an OER and its distinction between open and merely free materials.
    3. OER Resources for Educators - Creative Commons - A teacher's guide to finding openly licensed images, video, audio, textbooks, simulations, and courses, with pointers to OpenStax, PhET, and OER Commons. Supports the chapter's section on finding OER.

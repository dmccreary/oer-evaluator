    Quiz: Practice and Feedback¶

    Test your understanding of formative assessment, quiz design, feedback messages, retrieval and spaced practice, worked examples, and simulations with these review questions.

    1. What are the three parts of a multiple-choice question?¶

    1. The stem, the key, and the distractors
    2. The prompt, the rubric, and the answer key
    3. The stem, the feedback message, and the question bank
    4. The key, the outcome tag, and the retry control
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The stem is the question or incomplete statement, the key is the correct option, and the distractors are the wrong options. Multiple-choice questions are easy for a computer to score and fast to answer, but they test recognition more easily than the ability to produce an answer, so a good quiz includes other kinds too. A feedback message, a tag, and a retry control are useful extras and not parts of the question itself.

    See: Multiple-Choice Question

    2. Which term names practice in which the learner tries to recall something from memory without looking at the source?¶

    1. Spaced practice
    2. Massed practice
    3. Retrieval practice
    4. Summative assessment
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Retrieval practice includes answering a question, writing down what you remember of a chapter, and using flash cards. Research on the testing effect finds that retrieving information strengthens memory more than rereading it, and it works best when feedback follows. Spaced practice (A) and massed practice (B) describe when practice happens, not what the learner does, so a plan can be retrieval and spaced at once.

    See: Retrieval Practice

    3. How does a worked solution differ from a worked example?¶

    1. A worked example gives only final answers, while a worked solution gives every step of the method
    2. A worked example comes before the learner tries, as a model, while a worked solution comes after an attempt
    3. A worked example is written for teachers, while a worked solution is written for learners
    4. A worked example appears after the attempt, while a worked solution appears before it
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Both show a full step-by-step solution, and the difference is timing and purpose. A worked example comes before practice as a model of the method. A worked solution comes after the learner's attempt, so the learner can find where their own method went wrong. An answer key, by contrast, shows only the final answers (A). Option D reverses the order.

    Concept Tested: Worked Solution

    See: Worked Solution

    4. Which pair correctly contrasts formative and summative assessment?¶

    1. Formative assessment is always graded, while summative assessment is never graded at all
    2. Formative assessment comes at the end of a unit, while summative assessment happens during learning
    3. Formative assessment guides learning as it happens, while summative assessment judges what was learned at the end
    4. Formative assessment uses only multiple-choice items, while summative assessment uses only essays
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Formative assessment is assessment for learning: it is low stakes, often ungraded, repeatable, and used to adjust what comes next. Summative assessment, such as a final exam or a certificate exam, judges what was learned at the end. The same format can serve either purpose, so the question to ask is what the result is used for. A quiz in an open resource is usually a formative tool.

    See: Formative Assessment

    5. Why does the chapter say that a quiz ending with "You got 5 of 8" has not given the learner useful feedback?¶

    1. A score always arrives too late to affect what the learner does next, however soon it is shown to them
    2. Quizzes should never show scores to learners, because scores discourage them from trying again
    3. Feedback must always be delivered in person by a teacher, because software cannot explain
    4. A score says how the learner did but not why, so nothing explains or corrects a misunderstanding
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A feedback message is the part of practice that does the teaching. A strong message confirms or corrects the answer, says why, and, when the answer was wrong, names the likely misunderstanding and points to what to review. A total can hide that explanations are missing, so a reviewer opens each item and looks for a message. Online resources can show this feedback immediately and automatically, which print cannot match.

    6. Why do worked examples often help learners who are new to a topic more than solving the same problems unaided?¶

    1. Beginners learn more from guessing before they see any solution, because errors are memorable
    2. Solving from scratch uses attention that a beginner needs to see the method, so studying a full solution helps
    3. Worked examples remove the need for any practice problems, because the method is already shown
    4. Worked examples let learners skip feedback because the steps are already shown to them
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Research on the worked example effect, associated with John Sweller and colleagues, finds that novices often learn more from studying worked examples because problem solving from scratch takes the attention they need to see the method. The best examples show each step and the reason for it. As skill grows, examples can fade, leaving steps for the learner to fill in. Practice problems are still needed, because a worked example never lets the learner try the method.

    Concept Tested: Worked Example

    See: Worked Example

    7. A learner answers "Which describes infiltration?" with "Water flowing over the ground." Which feedback message best follows the chapter's guidance?¶

    1. "Not quite. That is runoff, which flows over the ground. Infiltration is water soaking in, so review the two."
    2. "Wrong. Try again, and this time read the question and all four options more carefully before you answer."
    3. "Incorrect. The answer is the first option, so mark it as correct and move on to the next question when you are ready."
    4. "Good try! Keep going, because you are doing well and the next question is easier than this one was."
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A strong message corrects the answer, explains why, names the likely mix-up, and points to what to review, without blaming the learner. Option A says that the chosen answer describes runoff and defines infiltration as its opposite. "Wrong" (B) and the bare answer (C) say what failed or what is right without a reason. "Good try" (D) does not even say whether the answer was correct, which makes it unhelpful.

    Concept Tested: Feedback Message

    See: Feedback Message

    8. A student closes the book and quizzes themself with flash cards on Monday, again on Thursday, and again the following Monday. How should this plan be classified?¶

    1. Retrieval practice, spaced across several days
    2. Rereading, spaced over several days with gaps between the sessions
    3. Retrieval practice, massed into one long sitting
    4. Rereading, massed into one sitting the night before
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Quizzing with the book closed is retrieval practice, because the learner tries to recall without looking. The gaps of several days between sessions make the practice spaced, and the spacing effect is among the most reliable findings in the study of learning. Retrieval and spacing are independent ideas: a plan can have one without the other. Cramming flash cards for three hours the evening before an exam would be retrieval that is massed (C).

    9. A reviewer reads this question: "Which caused increased runoff? A. Paving. B. Bare soil. C. Heavy rain. D. All of the above." What is the main flaw?¶

    1. The stem is written in the negative, which makes learners reverse their thinking before answering
    2. The key is noticeably longer than the other options, which gives the answer away
    3. A grammatical clue before the options points to the key, so learners can guess it
    4. "All of the above" lets a learner who knows two options are right guess the third without testing it
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The chapter lists seven common flaws in multiple-choice questions: an ambiguous stem, an implausible distractor, a longer key, an "all of the above" option, a negative stem, a grammatical clue, and a pattern in key positions. This question has none of the others. Each option is short, the stem is positive, and no article points to an answer. A learner who recognizes two correct options can guess the third without knowing anything about it.

    See: Multiple-Choice Question

    10. A reviewer finds that a unit has no practice questions at all, only one worked example. How does the chapter's scoring rule treat the other practice checks, and what is the effect on the score?¶

    1. Checks 2 to 5, 9, and 10 count as passed because they do not apply, so the score stays high
    2. Checks 2 to 5, 9, and 10 are skipped, and only check 7, the worked example, is scored
    3. Checks 2 to 5, 9, and 10 count as failed because check 1 fails, and the serious failure selects the lower number
    4. Checks 2 to 5, 9, and 10 count as failed, but the higher number applies because the worked example passes
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Check 1 asks whether the resource offers practice with feedback on at least some items, and it is the one serious check. When it fails, the checks that depend on practice existing, which are 2 to 5, 9, and 10, count as failed. In the chapter's Reading Only Unit only the worked example passes, a share of 10 percent, and the serious failure selects the lower number, so the score is 1. Practice with no feedback, or feedback with no practice, is not enough.

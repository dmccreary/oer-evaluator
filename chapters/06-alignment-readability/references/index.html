    References: Alignment and Readability¶

    1. Bloom's taxonomy - Wikipedia - Describes the original and revised Bloom's taxonomies and their six cognitive levels with example verbs. Supports the chapter's method of matching a resource's activities to the level of its stated outcomes.

    2. Flesch–Kincaid readability tests - Wikipedia - Gives the formulas for the Reading Ease and Grade Level tests, their history, and their limits. Background for the reading level checks in the readability metric.

    3. Constructive alignment - Wikipedia - Explains John Biggs's principle that outcomes, teaching activities, and assessment should match one another. The idea behind this chapter's alignment metric and its outcome-to-content mapping.

    4. Teaching for Quality Learning at University (4th ed.) - John Biggs and Catherine Tang - McGraw-Hill / Open University Press (2011) - Biggs introduced constructive alignment, asking teachers to write outcomes as verbs and then design activities and assessments that require those verbs. It is the basis of this chapter's outcome-to-content mapping.

    5. Understanding by Design (2nd expanded ed.) - Grant Wiggins and Jay McTighe - ASCD (2005) - Wiggins and McTighe's backward design starts with desired results, then evidence, then activities. A reviewer can follow the same sequence to judge whether a resource's content serves its stated outcomes.

    6. Plain Language Guide Series - Digital.gov - Federal guidance on writing short sentences, active voice, and familiar words, with examples. Gives concrete criteria for judging a resource's plain-language quality and text structure.

    7. Learning Objectives - Carnegie Mellon Eberly Center - Explains how to write measurable learning objectives with action verbs and how objectives shape assessment and activities. Helps reviewers judge whether a resource's stated objectives are testable.

    8. Common Core State Standards Initiative - CCSSI - Home of the K-12 English language arts and mathematics standards. Reviewers scoring a K-12 resource use these standards as the target when checking alignment.

    9. Readability Formulas - Readability Formulas - Explains common readability formulas and offers a free online test for pasted text. Lets reviewers check a passage's reading level quickly and see how the formulas differ.

    10. Legibility, Readability, and Comprehension: Making Users Read Your Words - Nielsen Norman Group - Separates how legible the type is, how readable the words are, and how well readers understand. A reminder that a reading-level score alone does not measure comprehension.

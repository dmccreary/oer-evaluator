    Quiz: The Ten-Metric Rubric and Scoring¶

    Test your understanding of the ten-metric rubric, metric scores, evidence, subtotals, reviewers, and rubric reliability with these review questions.

    1. How is the 100-point total of the ten-metric rubric divided?¶

    1. Five quality metrics and five reusability metrics, each worth 10 points
    2. Ten metrics with different weights, with accuracy worth the most points
    3. Two dimensions, with quality worth 70 points and reusability worth 30 points
    4. Seven quality metrics and three reusability metrics, each worth 10 points
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The ten-metric rubric scores five quality metrics and five reusability metrics, each worth 10 points, so the quality subtotal and the reusability subtotal are each worth 50 points and the total is 100. Equal weights keep scores easy to compare between resources and reviewers. The numbers on the worksheet show the order of the rows, not the importance of the metrics.

    2. What three parts does good evidence for a score contain?¶

    1. Where you looked, what you saw, and the rule you compared it with
    2. Who reviewed it, when it was reviewed, and the final score
    3. A quote, a screenshot, and a signature from a second reviewer
    4. The metric name, the score, and the resource's total
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Evidence gives the location, such as a page, section, URL, or file; the observation, such as a quote, count, or measurement; and the rule, which is the check from the metric. With those three parts another reviewer can repeat your check and reach the same observation. Opinions such as "the facts seem right" and unchecked claims by an AI agent are not evidence yet.

    3. Which of these metrics belongs to the reusability dimension of the rubric?¶

    1. Readability
    2. Accessibility
    3. Practice with feedback
    4. Editable source
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The five reusability metrics are open license, modularity, metadata, editable source, and portability. They measure how easily other people can find, change, and reuse a resource. Readability, accessibility, and practice with feedback belong to the quality dimension, along with accuracy and currency and alignment, because they measure how well the resource helps learners learn.

    4. Which statement correctly describes an analytic rubric?¶

    1. It gives one overall rating for the work, such as four stars
    2. It scores several criteria separately and then combines the scores
    3. It scores only the accuracy of the facts in a resource
    4. It lets an AI agent choose the criteria for each review
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. An analytic rubric scores several criteria separately and then combines them. It takes longer than a holistic rubric, which gives a single overall rating, but it shows what to fix and lets different reviewers compare their scores. That is why this book uses an analytic rubric with ten metrics. A holistic rating such as four stars hides which part of a resource failed a learner.

    5. Three resources each total 65 points, yet the chapter says they call for three different actions. Why?¶

    1. Totals are calculated by a different formula for each resource that is reviewed
    2. The total hides the split between the two halves and the weakest metric
    3. Each resource must be reviewed with a different rubric before its total can be used
    4. A total of 65 is not a valid score on this rubric, so the resources must have been scored wrongly
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The total is the sum of all ten metric scores, so it is the least informative number in a report. One resource may be weak on metadata, another locked by its license, and a third full of factual errors, yet all three total 65. Report the total together with the two subtotals and the lowest metric. Totals are most useful for comparing two versions of the same resource.

    6. What does it mean to say that a rubric is reliable but not valid?¶

    1. It measures what matters, but different reviewers get quite different scores for the same resource
    2. It produces scores only for resources that carry an open license and are written in English
    3. It gives consistent scores, but the scores may not measure what matters to learners
    4. It was written by experts, but no reviewer has ever used it to score a real resource
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Reliability is consistency: different reviewers, or the same reviewer later, produce similar scores. Validity is whether the scores measure what they claim to measure. A rubric can give the same wrong answer every time, like a scale that is steady but off. Option A describes the reverse case. Chapters 16 and 17 test validity by asking whether students learn more from resources that score higher.

    7. Why does the chapter recommend that a reviewer finish all ten scores before looking at a colleague's or an AI agent's scores?¶

    1. Scoring alone is faster because there are fewer metrics to check
    2. Reviewers should never discuss their scores with anyone else
    3. A colleague's score is likely to be wrong, while the reviewer's own is correct
    4. A number seen first pulls judgment toward it, a bias called anchoring
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Anchoring means the first number you see or write pulls later judgments toward it, and you will not feel it happen. Scoring alone first, with evidence, protects each reviewer's independent judgment. Reviewers then compare metric by metric and discuss only the metrics where scores differ by more than 3 points. Discussion is part of the method, so option B is wrong, and neither score is assumed correct (C).

    8. Why does a reviewer start by writing down the resource's intended audience and learning outcomes?¶

    1. The rubric awards 10 bonus points for each audience that a resource names in its description
    2. Quality depends on the learner, so the resource is scored against what it was built to do
    3. A resource without a stated audience cannot carry an open license, because licenses require an audience
    4. The reviewer must prove that the intended audience likes the resource before any score is given
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. OER quality is the degree to which a resource helps its intended learners reach its intended outcomes, so it describes a relationship between a resource and a learner. A chemistry reading that is excellent for a first-year college class can be poor quality for a grade 8 class. A resource that states neither audience nor outcomes has a quality problem before the review begins.

    9. A reviewer scores a resource: accuracy 8, alignment 8, readability 9, accessibility 7, and practice 8, then open license 9, modularity 8, metadata 3, editable source 8, and portability 9. What do the chapter's total and adoption rule give?¶

    1. Total 77, Adopt, because the total is at least 75 and so the rule is met
    2. Total 77, Reject, because one metric scores below 5 and that always rules out a resource
    3. Total 77, Revise, because one metric scores below the floor of 5
    4. Total 74, Revise, because the total is below 75 and the metadata score is not counted
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The quality scores add to 40 and the reusability scores add to 37, so the total is 77. That meets the total of 75, but metadata scores 3, which is below the floor of 5, so the Adopt rule is not met. The decision is Revise, because the total is at least 50. Reject applies only when the total is below 50. The floor stops one failed metric from hiding inside a high total.

    10. Two reviewers each total 65 for the same resource. Reviewer 1 scored accessibility 5 and portability 7, and Reviewer 2 scored accessibility 9 and portability 3. What does comparing the two worksheets show?¶

    1. Two metrics are flagged because each differs by more than 3, so the matching totals hide real disagreement
    2. The reviewers agree, because matching totals show that their scores are consistent on every metric
    3. Neither metric is flagged, because the two differences cancel out in the total and so do not count
    4. Only portability is flagged, because the reviewer who gave the lower score must justify that score
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A metric agrees when the two scores differ by 3 points or fewer, and it is flagged when they differ by more than 3. Accessibility differs by 4 and portability differs by 4, so both are flagged even though the totals are identical. Compare reviewers metric by metric, then examine the evidence for each flagged metric. Reviewer 2 may have missed the video without captions, or Reviewer 1 may have missed a platform where the embed works.

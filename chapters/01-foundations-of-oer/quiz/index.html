    Quiz: Foundations of Open Educational Resources¶

    Test your understanding of open educational resources, the 5R permissions, and the ways teachers adopt, adapt, and create OER with these review questions.

    1. Which statement best defines an open educational resource (OER)?¶

    1. Any educational resource that the user can read or download without paying
    2. Any complete textbook that a university makes available to read online
    3. Any educational resource that has passed a peer review by its publisher
    4. An educational resource that is in the public domain or carries an open license
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. An OER is an educational resource that is in the public domain or carries an open license, so anyone may use, adapt, and share it without asking first. Option A describes a free resource, which may still forbid copying and editing. Options B and C describe a source and a quality check, and the definition requires neither. Openness is about permission, not about price, publisher, or approval.

    2. Under U.S. law, when does copyright protection for a new work begin?¶

    1. When the creator registers the work with the Copyright Office
    2. Automatically, when the work is fixed in a tangible form such as a saved file
    3. When the creator adds a © symbol and an "all rights reserved" notice
    4. When the creator publishes the work for sale or posts it online
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Protection begins the moment a work is fixed in a tangible form, such as a typed document, a saved file, or a recording. Registration, a © symbol, and publication are not required. This is why a worksheet on a blog with no license statement is still protected, and why a teacher should treat it as all rights reserved unless the owner grants permission.

    3. Which list names the 5R permissions that an open license can grant?¶

    1. Read, reuse, rewrite, resell, redistribute
    2. Retain, reproduce, revise, resell, redistribute
    3. Retain, reuse, revise, remix, redistribute
    4. Reproduce, adapt, distribute, perform, display
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. David Wiley named the 5R permissions retain, reuse, revise, remix, and redistribute. Option D is a tempting mix-up because it lists the five exclusive rights that copyright gives an owner: reproduce, adapt, distribute, perform, and display. Those are what an owner controls by default, while the 5Rs are what an open license hands to everyone else. Options A and B swap in words that are not part of the framework.

    4. What makes a material an educational resource?¶

    1. Its format, because only textbooks, articles, and readings count as resources
    2. Its price, because a resource must be free of charge to be used in teaching
    3. Its purpose, because any material that supports teaching and learning counts, whatever its format
    4. Its publisher, because the material must come from a school or a university
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. An educational resource is any material that a teacher or learner uses to support teaching and learning. The definition depends on purpose and not on format: a photograph of a canyon is a family snapshot in an album, and it becomes an educational resource when a teacher uses it to explain erosion. Texts, media, tools, assessments, and plans all qualify, so format (A), price (B), and publisher (D) do not decide it.

    5. Why is a publisher's free sample chapter marked "All rights reserved" not an OER?¶

    1. It is only an excerpt, and OER must be complete books
    2. It costs nothing but grants no permission to edit or share it
    3. It comes from a company, and OER must come from universities
    4. It is protected by copyright, and OER cannot be copyrighted by their creators
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The sample chapter passes the price test but fails the permission test, because "All rights reserved" grants no permission to revise, remix, or redistribute it. OER can be a single worksheet, so length and source do not matter (A and C). OER are usually copyrighted too: an open license is a permission the owner grants on a protected work, so D reverses how openness works.

    6. Under the 5R framework, how does the revise permission differ from the remix permission?¶

    1. Revise lets you share a work with others, while remix allows private use only
    2. Revise applies to text only, while remix applies to images and video
    3. Revise requires the author's approval each time, while remix does not
    4. Revise changes a single work, while remix combines two or more works into something new
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Revising means adapting one work: fixing an error, translating it, or adding alt text. Remixing means joining the work with other open material, such as a unit built from chapters of two open textbooks. Echo's rule of thumb is to ask whether you changed one work or combined several. Sharing copies is the redistribute permission, so option A confuses two of the five. Neither permission is limited by media type or needs fresh approval.

    7. A teacher downloads an openly licensed textbook's PDF and its editable source files to her own laptop so that she still has them if the website goes offline. Which of the 5R permissions is she using?¶

    1. Retain, because she is making and keeping her own copy
    2. Reuse, because she is using the work in its original form
    3. Revise, because the source files let her change the work
    4. Redistribute, because she is moving copies between devices
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The retain permission covers downloading, duplicating, storing, and managing your own copy. Websites change and disappear, and a bookmark is not a copy, so keeping the files protects next year's lesson. She has not yet changed the work (revise), taught with it (reuse), or given copies to others (redistribute). The test question for retain is, "Can I make and keep my own copy?"

    8. Why does the chapter say that adopting an open textbook still requires evaluating it?¶

    1. The license shows whether you may use the book, but not whether the book is good
    2. Adopted resources must be revised before any student is allowed to read them
    3. Open licenses expire unless the adopter checks the resource and renews the license each year
    4. Adopting an open textbook transfers its copyright to the adopting teacher
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The license answers "may I use it?", while the question "is it good?" stays open until someone checks. An open text may be out of date, may assume prior knowledge that your students lack, or may have no practice problems. Adoption means using the resource as it is, so revision is optional (B). Open licenses do not expire when unchecked (C), and adoption never transfers copyright (D). This gap is why the book teaches a rubric.

    9. In open pedagogy, how does a renewable assignment differ from a disposable assignment?¶

    1. A renewable assignment is repeated every term by the same teacher, while a disposable one is given only once and never again
    2. A renewable assignment is graded by classmates, while a disposable assignment is graded by the teacher alone
    3. A renewable assignment uses a commercial textbook for its readings, while a disposable assignment uses an open textbook
    4. A renewable assignment creates lasting value that others can reuse, while a disposable one is written, graded, and discarded
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A disposable assignment is written, graded, and thrown away. A renewable assignment adds lasting value: for example, each anatomy student writes and illustrates one section of an open textbook that later cohorts revise. The word "renewable" refers to the work's lasting life, not to repeating the task (A) or to who grades it (B). Because students own their work, they must choose to release it openly.

    10. A department announces, "Our course is a zero textbook cost course because the library licenses the e-books, so it is built on OER." What is wrong with this reasoning?¶

    1. Zero textbook cost describes price and OER describes permissions, so free library e-books can be zero cost without being open
    2. Zero textbook cost courses may use only printed books and never e-books, because e-books always carry a fee
    3. Library e-books are always in the public domain, so they need no license and no permission at all
    4. OER must carry a price so that the open license can be funded, and a free course cannot be built on OER
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A ZTC course means students pay nothing for required materials, and it often relies on OER, but it need not. Library-licensed e-books are free to read yet typically forbid copying, editing, and sharing, so they are free but not open. The department has confused the price dial with the permission dial. E-books are allowed in ZTC courses (B), library e-books are rarely public domain (C), and OER have no price requirement (D).

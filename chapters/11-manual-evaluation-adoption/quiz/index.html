    Quiz: Manual Evaluation and Adoption Decisions¶

    Test your understanding of the manual review workflow, calibration, consensus scoring, and the adopt, revise, or reject decision with these review questions.

    1. Which step comes first in the nine-step manual review workflow?¶

    1. Read the whole resource once, without scoring, to see the full picture
    2. Score one metric at a time, writing the evidence in the same row as you go
    3. Give the worksheet to a second reviewer who has already scored the resource alone
    4. Save a dated copy of the resource and fill in the resource block of the worksheet
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The workflow begins with a dated copy and the resource block, because the evidence must refer to a fixed version and a web page can change the next day. The checklist and tools come next, then one unscored read of the whole resource, then scoring one metric at a time. Only after the subtotals and total does the worksheet go to a second reviewer, followed by reconciliation, the decision, and the report.

    2. Who makes an adoption decision, and what is the role of the review?¶

    1. A person or group with authority, such as a department committee, who weigh the review against cost and fit
    2. The reviewer alone, because the total score decides the matter automatically and cannot be changed
    3. The resource's author, who accepts or rejects the reviewer's findings before the report is final
    4. An AI agent, which converts the total score into a decision and records it for the district
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A review informs the decision and does not make it. The deciding group weighs the review against things the rubric does not measure, such as cost, fit with the course, and training needs. A good decision is recorded with the date, the decision-makers, the review it relied on, and a time to look again, because a resource that scores well today may be out of date in a few years.

    3. How do reviewer notes differ from evidence?¶

    1. Notes are shared with the resource's author, while evidence stays private to the review team
    2. Notes are written after the review, while evidence is written before it
    3. Notes are always correct, while evidence may turn out to be wrong
    4. Evidence is a checked observation that supports a score, while a note is a lead that may become evidence
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Reviewer notes are the informal jottings made during a review: questions, hunches, and things to check later. Keeping them apart from the evidence column stops an unchecked hunch from posing as a finding. A note may turn into evidence once it is checked, or it may prove wrong. Many reviewers keep a short "to check" list and clear it before they finish.

    4. What does an adoption recommendation contain?¶

    1. Only the total score, the score band, and the date on which the whole review was finished
    2. The decision the scores support, the reasons, the strongest and weakest metrics, and the best improvements
    3. The full evidence column for all ten metrics, with no summary or advice for the reader
    4. The author's response to the review and a plan to rewrite the resource from the beginning
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. An adoption recommendation is the reviewer's written advice to the decision-makers. It is the part of the review that busy readers will actually read, so it comes first in the report and fits on a page. The chapter's example for Watershed Basics runs three sentences: revise before adopting, the score and the reason the rule is not met, and the three changes that would help most.

    5. Why does the chapter say that consensus scoring should not average two reviewers' scores?¶

    1. Averages always round up and so inflate the final score of every resource that is reviewed
    2. Averaging is allowed only for the quality metrics and not for the reusability metrics
    3. An average such as 7 from a 5 and a 9 hides the disagreement and gives a number nobody found
    4. Averaging is allowed only when the reviewers differ by more than 3 points
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Consensus scoring means two or more reviewers agree on one final score for each metric by deciding on the evidence. Averaging feels fair and tells you nothing. When reviewers cannot agree, they record both scores and the reason, and the report says the metric is disputed. The final score is the one that the checked evidence supports, whether or not it lies between the two original scores.

    6. Why does the chapter have readers score a resource by hand before they use an AI agent?¶

    1. A reviewer who knows a correct review can check an agent's work, and the manual review is the reference
    2. AI agents cannot read open educational resources, so humans must always score them by hand
    3. A manual review is always faster than a review by an agent, whatever the size of the resource
    4. Agents may be used only after the adoption decision has been made by the committee
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Manual evaluation is slower than letting software do it, and it is the reference against which software is measured. Reading a resource carefully also surfaces things that no automated check will. You cannot check an agent's evidence in later chapters unless you know what a correct review looks like. The product of a manual evaluation is a completed worksheet, a total, a decision, and a report.

    7. A resource has a total score of exactly 50 and a lowest metric score of 3. What decision does the chapter's rule give?¶

    1. Reject, because 50 is too low to adopt and the Reject rule applies at exactly 50
    2. Adopt, because the total is at least 50 and that meets the starting rule for adoption
    3. Revise, because the total is not below 50 and the Adopt rule is not met
    4. Revise, but only if a second reviewer agrees with the score and confirms the total
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The rule has sharp edges. Adopt needs a total of at least 75 and every metric at least 5. Reject applies only when the total is below 50, so a total of exactly 50 is not a Reject, and 49 is. A resource at 50 that does not meet the Adopt rule is a Revise: promising, and in need of work. The decision follows from the numbers and does not depend on a second reviewer's agreement (D).

    8. Two reviewers score accessibility for the same resource. Reviewer A gives a 5 and records that 4 of 5 images have alt text, two of those are one word, and the video has no captions. Reviewer B gives a 9 and records that an automated checker reported no errors. How should they resolve the metric?¶

    1. Average the two scores to 7, because the evidence from each reviewer is equally good
    2. Keep A's score, because A's evidence is specific and repeatable and a checker cannot judge alt text quality
    3. Keep Reviewer B's score, because a tool report settles any score without any further checking
    4. Record the disagreement without repeating any check, and let the decision-makers choose a score
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Score reconciliation has three steps: each reviewer states the evidence, the reviewers repeat the check that the evidence rests on, and they choose the score that the checked evidence supports. A's evidence names counts and a missing feature that anyone can verify. B's evidence does not address those findings, since a checker can pass alt text that says "photo." A score backed by repeatable evidence outweighs a vague impression.

    9. The Watershed Basics review totals 65, and its lowest metric is metadata at 4. Which analysis best explains the recommendation "Revise before adopting"?¶

    1. The total is above 75, but metadata is below the floor of 5, which blocks adoption
    2. The total is below 50, so the resource is rejected without any chance of revision
    3. Every metric is at least 5, but the total is too low to adopt the resource
    4. The total is below 75 and metadata is below the floor of 5, so both parts of the Adopt rule fail
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The Adopt rule has two parts: a total of at least 75 and every metric at least 5. Watershed Basics fails both, with 65 and a metadata score of 4. Reject needs a total below 50, so the resource lands in Revise. Most findings are cheap to fix: filling in the metadata, adding captions and alt text, and separating the quiz would each take less time than writing the resource did.

    10. Four reviewers flag 5 of 10 metrics when they score sample A together. After discussing and writing down shared readings, they score sample B and flag 1 metric. How should the team interpret this?¶

    1. Calibration failed, because the number of flagged metrics changed between the two samples
    2. The shared readings brought agreement within 3 points on 9 of 10 metrics, which meets the goal, so scoring can begin
    3. The team must repeat the session with a new sample, because any flagged metric means failure
    4. The team should average the reviewers' scores to remove the one remaining flagged metric
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A calibration session turns a written rubric into a shared one. A reasonable goal, which a team may change, is that reviewers agree within 3 points on at least 8 of the 10 metrics before they score real resources. One flagged metric means 9 of 10 agree, so the goal is met. Only when the goal is missed does the group repeat the session with a new sample. Averaging (D) is not a way to resolve a flag.

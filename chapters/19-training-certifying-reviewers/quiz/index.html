    Quiz: Training Teachers and Certifying Reviewers¶

    Test your understanding of workshop design, calibration exercises, certificate review, training evaluation, the local reviewer role, and the capstone project with these review questions.

    1. Within how many days of the workshop must a teacher submit the certificate evidence?¶

    1. 7 days
    2. 14 days
    3. 30 days
    4. 90 days
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The fifth certificate requirement is to submit both the evaluation and the improved component within 30 days of the workshop. For a training day on February 20, 2027, the sample timeline sets the deadline at March 22, 2027. A teacher should submit early enough to leave time for a revision, because a reviewer may ask for one. The other four requirements cover attendance, the calibration exercise, the evaluation with evidence, and a published CC BY component.

    2. Which list gives the four levels of the Kirkpatrick model that the chapter uses to evaluate a training?¶

    1. Reaction, learning, behavior, and results
    2. Reach, reuse, learning, and experience
    3. Attendance, completion, certificate, and publication
    4. Plan, do, check, and act
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A training evaluation asks how participants reacted, what they learned, whether their behavior changed afterward, and what results followed. An exit survey measures only the first level. Judging a program needs evidence at every level, and the later levels take more time to collect. Option B lists the four kinds of evidence used in an impact report in Chapter 18, which is a related but different framework.

    3. Why does the sample workshop agenda place the session on checking the agent before the improvement challenge?¶

    1. Teachers need a lunch break between the two sessions, and the agenda gives one there
    2. Chapter 14 showed that an unchecked agent should not be trusted to change anything
    3. The improvement challenge uses fewer tools than the checking session does, so it can come second
    4. The certificate checklist forbids any other order of these two sessions in a workshop
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Each session depends on a product of an earlier one. Setup comes first because nothing works without a running agent. The rubric precedes calibration, calibration precedes checking because you need your own scores to judge the agent's, and checking precedes improving. Peer review and publishing come last because they need a finished component. A good agenda puts the sessions in an order where each one prepares the next.

    4. What does a facilitator guide give a colleague who did not write the workshop?¶

    1. Only the slides for the short talks, with no notes for the facilitator to follow
    2. The participants' certificates, ready to be signed and handed out at the end of the day
    3. A list of the teachers who attended, with their names and the resource each one brought
    4. For each session, the time, goal, materials, steps, key points, common problems with fixes, and answers
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A facilitator who wrote the workshop can run it from memory, and a colleague who did not needs a guide. The most useful part is the list of common problems with fixes, because that is where an unprepared facilitator loses time. For example, if a teacher wants to paste a class roster into the agent, the guide tells the facilitator to stop it and use the sample quiz, since student data stays out of prompts.

    5. In the calibration exercise, a teacher scores Accessibility 5 and the agent scores it 9. What should the teacher do?¶

    1. Flag it, because 4 is more than 3, then trace it to the evidence and record which score is trusted
    2. Average the two scores to 7 and move on, since the difference is only one band
    3. Accept the agent's score, because the agent works faster than a teacher can
    4. Ignore the difference, because it is within the agreement threshold and so needs no further comment
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The worksheet asks teachers to list every metric on which their score and the agent's differ by more than 3 points and to explain which score they trust. Here the agent said contrast was fine with no tool log, and the video has no captions, so the teacher trusts the 5. Averaging (B) hides the disagreement, and a gap of 4 exceeds the threshold of 3 (D). Explaining one disagreement from the evidence teaches more than agreeing on nine.

    6. A certificate submission is complete and published under CC BY, with gains on two quality metrics and on only one reusability metric. What should the reviewer decide?¶

    1. Certify, because most of the requirements are met and the evidence is complete
    2. Not eligible, because a requirement is unmet and the submission cannot be repaired
    3. Certify, with a note asking for a later improvement on the missing reusability gain
    4. Revise and resubmit, because a gain on a second reusability metric is still needed
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The fourth requirement is a published CC BY component that gains on at least two quality and two reusability metrics. A submission with one gap that can be fixed is returned with the specific gap named, so the teacher can revise and resubmit. Not eligible is for a requirement that revision cannot meet, such as missing the training day. Effort and attendance alone do not earn the certificate, and the decision rests on evidence in the submission.

    7. A submission's findings log lists six agent findings, all marked "accepted," with no reasons given. What does the pattern suggest?¶

    1. The agent made no errors, so the submission can be certified at once
    2. The findings log is optional and can be skipped when the agent's report looks clean
    3. The teacher checked the agent especially well and recorded only the final decisions
    4. There is no sign that the agent's output was checked, so the reviewer should ask for verification
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A log in which every agent finding was accepted without reasons suggests that nobody tested the claims. The third requirement asks for a note on every agent finding accepted, corrected, or rejected, and the review step is to read the findings log. Agents make confident mistakes, as Chapter 14 showed, so a clean record with no reasons is a warning. The reviewer asks for the verification and does not certify on trust.

    8. A workshop reports that 18 of 19 respondents rated the day 4 or 5 and that 17 of 20 teachers earned the certificate. Which analysis is best?¶

    1. Both figures show results, which is the top level of the model, since both are counts
    2. The ratings show reaction and the certificates show learning, but not that behavior or results changed
    3. Both figures show that behavior changed after the workshop, since teachers are using the skills
    4. Both figures show reaction only, since both come from the participants themselves
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. An exit survey measures how participants felt about the day, which is reaction. A certificate shows that skills were demonstrated, which is learning. Behavior needs evidence such as teachers scoring another resource within three months, and results need evidence such as published components being reused by others. Real evaluation also faces the cautions of Chapters 16 and 17: without a comparison group a change may have other causes.

    9. A certified teacher is asked to decide that a resource's license is legally safe to publish across the district. What is the best judgment?¶

    1. It is outside the local reviewer's role, because legal questions go to the school's counsel
    2. It is inside the role, because the teacher can read licenses and flag problems
    3. It is inside the role, because the teacher holds the certificate and has been trained for the role
    4. It is inside the role if the agent agrees with the teacher's reading of the license
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A local reviewer reads a license statement and flags problems, and does not rule on what is legally safe. The role has four limits: no legal rulings, no student information in prompts, no adoption decision made alone, and no certifying of other teachers. Inside the role are scoring with the rubric and an agent, verifying the agent's claims, keeping a findings log, and telling a colleague which metric to fix first. The reviewer repeats a calibration exercise at least once a year.

    10. A teacher is planning a capstone project. Which plan best meets the capstone's four tests?¶

    1. Improve one metric of a resource that the teacher likes, publish it online under a license, and share a link without any report
    2. Evaluate a resource and improve it, skip the student test because testing is optional, publish it, and announce the gain
    3. Evaluate a resource, check the agent, improve two quality and two reusability metrics, test with students, publish under CC BY, and report honestly
    4. Improve four metrics, test with a few students without asking permission, publish under CC BY-NC, and share the link as the report
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The capstone uses every skill in the book on one resource from your own subject. It succeeds when it improves at least two quality and two reusability metrics with evidence, includes a test with students that follows the consent and privacy rules, is published under CC BY with metadata, and reports the gain, the limits of the test, and any side effect honestly. Testing with students is required, and the capstone ends with sharing what was found, including an offer to train a colleague.

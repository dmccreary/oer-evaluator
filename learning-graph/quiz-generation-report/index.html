    How the Quality Score Is Computed¶

    The score is computed by script from mechanical checks only. It does not measure whether a question is ambiguous, and a human read of the questions is still the best check of that.

    Component Points How it is scored
    Format, links, and anchors 20 All 190 questions pass the format check, and every See: link points to an existing heading
    Answer balance 15 Full points when each letter is correct 2 or 3 times in the chapter
    Bloom's distribution 25 Reduced by the number of questions that are off the chapter type's target mix
    Concept coverage 20 Distinct concepts tested, relative to the smaller of 10 and the chapter's concept count
    Explanation length 10 Share of explanations between 50 and 110 words
    Distractor length balance 10 Reduced when the correct option is both the longest and 25 percent or more above the average distractor

    Distractor Length Review¶

    The first drafts made the correct option the longest in 148 of 190 questions, and in 130 of them the correct option was at least 25 percent longer than the average distractor. That is a cue that a test-wise student can use without knowing the content, and Chapter 8 of this book teaches reviewers to flag it. Five passes of revision lengthened the distractors, shortened over-long correct options, and rotated which option is longest.

    After revision, the correct option is the longest in 81 of 190 questions, the correct option is at least 25 percent longer than the average distractor in 1 questions, and the correct option averages 1.06 times the length of the average distractor. The correct option is the longest in no more than 5 of 10 questions in any chapter.

    Explanation Quality¶

    Explanations run from 59 to 91 words and average 79 words. Every question has an explanation that confirms the correct option, a Concept Tested label that matches a concept in the chapter's own table, and a See link to a heading in the chapter. Many explanations also say why a distractor is wrong.

    Recommendations¶

    1. Read the quizzes as a student would. The checks above are mechanical. An author's read for ambiguity and for a second defensible answer is the check that matters most, and it has not been done.
    2. Consider a second quiz for long chapters. Chapters 2, 3, 6, 7, 9, 12, 14, 15, and 18 list 22 or more concepts, and ten questions test fewer than half of them. A bank of 15 to 20 questions per chapter would test more, and the question bank would let a site draw a different ten each time.
    3. Review the Evaluate and Create questions. Only 9 of 190 questions are at those levels, because the target mix for chapters 4 to 13 allows none. The course description lists Evaluate and Create outcomes, and a multiple-choice question tests them only in part. Pair these quizzes with the improvement challenge and the capstone.
    4. Add difficulty data after use. The difficulty field in quiz-bank.json is derived from the Bloom level. After students take the quizzes, replace it with the share of students who answered correctly.
    5. Keep the quizzes in step with the chapters. The Concept Tested labels and See links depend on the chapter headings, so re-check them, and run mkdocs build --strict, after a chapter is edited.
